# Memory Planet

24h hackathon project: a journaling app where each memory spawns a building/object (and each new person a character) on a low-poly, spherical, inspectable toy planet. Plan: see `docs/CONTRACT.md` for interfaces.

## Run
`node server.js` then open http://localhost:8000 (must be served over HTTP; `file://` blocks GLB loading). Optional `.env` with `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=...` (never commit it).

## Deploy
Live on Vercel from `main`: it serves the repo as static files and runs `api/` as serverless functions, so **`server.js` is local-only** and is not deployed. The Claude call lives in `api/_classify.js`, shared by `server.js` and `api/classify.js` — change it in one place. `vercel.json` states outright that there is no framework, no build and no install step (without it a deploy answered the page itself with `FUNCTION_INVOCATION_FAILED`, having read `server.js` as a server entrypoint). Set `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` in the Vercel project's environment variables; without it the endpoint 503s and the browser falls back to the keyword classifier.

## Conventions
- **Vanilla Three.js r128 from the local `three-r128.min.js`, no build step, no ES modules.** Plain `<script>` tags in `index.html`, everything attached to the global `MI` namespace. Do not introduce npm packages, bundlers, React or R3F.
- `vendor/GLTFLoader.js` provides `THREE.GLTFLoader` (r128's CDN bundle doesn't include it).
- Kenney assets (CC0) in `assets/<pack>/`, keep each pack's textures next to its GLBs. The hexagon kit uses `Textures/variation-a.png`; its GLBs' colormap URI is redirected with `LoadingManager.setURLModifier`.
- **Loading a texture or model from a pack for the first time? Delete that pack's line from `.vercelignore` in the same commit.** Only `kenney-hexagon-kit` is deployed; the other packs are ignored so deploys stay small (4.6MB, not 38MB). A pack left listed there loads fine locally and 404s in production, which you will not notice until after deploying. Each pack also has its own atlas, so it needs its own `LoadingManager` and won't pick up the theme recolouring.
- Placement and asset choice are **persisted** in the Memory (never `Math.random()` at spawn time) so reloads reproduce the world. Use `MI.world` sphere helpers, not ad-hoc trig.
- Keep the file-owner boundaries in `docs/CONTRACT.md`. Small commits, merge to `main` often.
- Style: toy-diorama, low-poly, flat shading, warm palette; Quicksand font; charm over realism.
- The demo runs live on one laptop, possibly offline: every network feature (Claude classify) needs a working fallback.

## Status (2026-09-19)
Core loop is built and working: journal text -> classify -> people/slot/asset -> persist -> spawn on a 1002-tile hex-sphere (frequency 10), with terrain/landscape tiles, roads, procedural minifigure people, a flat-map view toggle, building swap in the detail panel, demo seed and reset. Persistence reproduces the world on reload.

Progression (built 2026-09-19): worlds start on the smallest planet (42 tiles) and grow up the size ladder 42 -> 92 -> 162 -> 362 -> 642 -> 1002 once half the land is claimed (`src/world/growth.js`; grids in `data/grids/`, f=10 stays `data/hexgrid.json`). Growing remaps every saved slot and keeps tile size constant, so the island keeps its size while new ocean opens around it. Journaling earns coins (`src/game/economy.js`), spent in the shop on 4 themes, 8 pets, 4 satellites and 5 character skins (visuals in `src/world/themes.js`, `src/world/cosmetics.js` and `src/world/walkers.js`). Store is v4 (`memory-planet.world.v4`); a v3 save migrates by splitting its `pets` into pets and satellites, a v2 save additionally as frequency 10. Checks: `node scripts/test-growth.js`.

Island view (was the flat view): the planet's land is coiled into a compact floating island rather than unrolled tile-for-tile, because the land grows as a winding chain and pressing it flat gave a strip. `src/world/island.js` (pure, `node scripts/test-island.js`) places each tile outward from home beside a planet neighbour, on the free cell nearest the middle, and re-routes the roads across the island (cheapest path, reusing paved cells). The land sits on a block of sea built per tile in `buildIslandUnderside` (deepest in the middle, a thin band of earth under the grass, the rest running the planet’s own water shader with `aLand` 0), with a soft shadow far below. The island hangs in a painted sky dome (`makeSky`/`makeSkyTexture`, per-theme `skyTop`/`skyBottom`/`clouds`, drawn wider than the starfield) that fades in with the view. Switching views is animated: the camera turns to home, tiles lift off the sphere and gather into the island while the planet shrinks away, and the sea grows down once they land (`makeFoldRig`, `unfoldToFlat`/`foldToPlanet`); the dithered cross-fade, lighting blend and mid-flight input lock are unchanged. Buttons read "island view" / "planet view".

Assets (merged 2026-09-19 from `asset_packs_and_textures`): Kenney packs plus `assets/standalone/`, a staging area of individual objects sorted by kind. GLB and textures only; `.fbx`/`.obj`/`.mtl` copies were dropped. The app loads the hexagon kit, Cube Pets, Mini Characters and the standalone buildings; see `.vercelignore` before loading any other pack.

Building variety (2026-09-20): the kit's eighteen buildings used to be **partitioned** across the six categories, three or four each, and the keyword guess sends most entries to `other` — so a planet showed the same three models over and over. A category now *prefers* its own buildings (`SIGNATURE_CHANCE`, 0.6) and draws from every building the rest of the time, which takes a category from 3 distinct models to 16-17 while a farm still means home. Four buildings from the pirate kit join them (two complete towers, the watch tower and the castle gate), which meant three things worth knowing:

- **LOOK AT A MODEL BEFORE CATALOGUING IT.** `assets/standalone/` is sorted by folder name, not by inspection, and `buildings/` holds component parts: the fantasy-town "windmill" is its sails alone — two crossed poles, no mill — and both "watermills" are a bare wheel. All three were catalogued and planted on the island before anyone rendered them, and they reached the player. **`/asset-sheet/` exists to make that a ten-second check**: it draws every GLB in a folder with its real dimensions underneath (`asset-sheet/`, listing served by `/api/assets` in the local-only `server.js`). The hexagon kit's own `building-mill.glb` and `building-watermill.glb` are the real mills.
- Retiring a model is safe: a building is persisted on its memory, so `spawnMemory` re-picks and re-saves any key that is no longer in the catalogue. Without that, a saved world asks for a file that is no longer served and the tile just comes up empty with nothing to say why.

- **A pack per loader, resolved from the url.** `loaderFor(dir)` keeps one `GLTFLoader` per pack directory, the directory being the url up to the file name — every pack keeps `Textures/` beside its GLBs, which is what makes that work. `PACKS` maps a pack name to its folder and `assetFor(key)` stamps the right one onto a Memory, so `{pack, key}` on a memory has finally started meaning something.
- **A hexagon-kit building includes the ground it stands on; a foreign one does not.** One kit model is the whole tile, base plate included. On the island a foreign building would therefore stand over a hole, so its tile is laid as plain grass and the model is set on top (`placeForeignOnIsland`). On the planet nothing was needed — the cell itself is the ground there.
- **Scale from what lands on the tile, not from the raw GLB box.** A model's box counts parts that never read as height, so raw numbers mislead by up to 2x. Measure against a hexagon-kit building's 0.52 model units above its tile; these sit at 1.1-1.5x that (0.70-0.75). The pirate tower scaled so its footprint fit the tile came out **2.9x** and had to come down by half.
- Known and accepted: the four foreign models **do not follow the theme**, since recolouring is an HSL pass over the hexagon kit's own atlas. Under Frostfall they stay summery. Fine for four; revisit before it is fifty.


Pets and satellites (built 2026-09-19, split 2026-09-19): **pets** are the ones that walk on the land, **satellites** are the ones that orbit in the sky. They are two separate kinds with their own shop tab and their own equip slot, so a world can have one of each out at once, and they are expected to grow apart from here — put anything new in whichever list matches where the thing actually lives.

- **Pets** (`src/world/walkers.js`, `MI.world.walkers`, `MI.world.setPet`) load Kenney Cube Pets GLBs and wander tile-to-tile in both views. The eight choices are bunny, pig, dog, fox, cow, deer, lion and elephant. Adding another means an entry in the walker models and the economy catalog.
- **Satellites** (`src/world/cosmetics.js`, `MI.world.setSatellite`) are the four procedural orbiters — moonling, cloud sheep, sky koi, tiny saucer. They live in the scene rather than on the planet, and `animateSatellite` eases them between circling the planet and circling the island by `state.viewMix`, so they keep flying through a change of view.
- One bug worth remembering: the island view's coiled layout (`MI.island`) isn't a 1:1 mirror of the planet's tile adjacency, so a pet's next step there has to come from `MI.island.adjacent` on the coiled cells, not the planet's own neighbour list — using the planet's neighbours there let the pet visually hop across a tile it was never really adjacent to.
- Known, pre-dating the split: on a one- or two-tile island the camera frames so tightly that a satellite's fixed island height puts it just above the top of the frame. `SATELLITE_ISLAND_HEIGHT` in `world.js` is the knob.

The pirate fleet (built 2026-09-20): ships sail the planet's ocean, start hostile, and are **won by writing** — the one thing on this planet you cannot buy, which is the point of it. `src/world/ships.js` is pure (who the ships are, what each asks for, whether it is earned; `node scripts/test-ships.js`), world.js sails them, app.js watches for a goal being met.

- **A ship is a walker whose walkable set is water.** That single substitution is the whole feature: `walkers.js` gained a `ship` kind and `makeWalkerSolo` (one instance, not a pair), and world.js passes the water tiles where it passes land tiles for a pet. No new movement code, because world.js was already the thing that decides where a walker may go.
- **Hostile means distance, not damage.** An unclaimed ship may only sit on OPEN SEA — water with no land neighbour — so it will not come near your island; a claimed one may use any water and sails straight in to the coast. Measured: unclaimed ships spent 0 of 400 sampled frames on a coastal tile, the claimed one 35 of 72. That is the whole difference between friend and foe, and it is one ternary in `updateShips`.
- **Goals are journalling milestones** (3 travel memories, a day with 2 people in it, a 3-day streak, 6 memories, 4 people met), counted from the world itself rather than tallied as things happen — so a demo seed or an import earns them the same as live writing. Claiming is the player's own click: a ship that flipped by itself would be a notification, where one you go and take is a moment. The hull swaps pirate -> civilian, which reads across the ocean without any text.
- **One announcement per entry**, even when an entry finishes two ships: the toast is a single element, so a second toast replaces the first and telling the player about two ships tells them about one.
- **A ship sits IN the water, and the water moves.** Two things were wrong in the first pass and both read as "floating": it was held at a fixed radius, but `animateWater` displaces every water tile along its own normal, so a fixed height rides over the troughs — `shipHeight` now recomputes that same wave and blends it across the two tiles a ship is between. And a walker with no animation clips gets HOP_HEIGHT, the little bob standing in for a walk cycle, which on a ship is skipping across the sea; ships pass `hop: 0`. On top of that `SHIP_DRAFT` (1.3 in model units, measured off the mesh — the keel is at 0 and the hull reaches full beam at 1.75, which is the deck) sinks the hull so the waterline, not the keel, meets the sea. Checked by raycasting the planet's own displaced surface under a ship: the keel sits 0.06-0.08 below it and never above.
- Ships are **planet-only**. The island is land coiled into a chunk with sea built *under* the tiles, so there is no open water to sail on; they hide during the fold rather than freezing, or a ship would be left standing on grass.
- Nothing about a ship's position is stored: only `{id, hull, name, goal, claimed}` in `world.ships`, with everything else rebuilt from the world's seed. `normalize()` backfills the field, so no version bump.

The journal (built 2026-09-19): a memory's fields are **asked for, not guessed**. The tag row under the entry box takes who was there (chips autocompleting from `world.people`, carrying `personId` so the same name is the same person), how it felt (five faces -> the stored `mood` shape), what kind (the six categories) and a big-day toggle (`importance` 4). `MI.ai.guess(text)` — the keyword heuristic, now word-boundary matched, `node scripts/test-guess.js` — only pre-selects, and never overwrites a control the writer touched. `MI.app.addEntry(text, { tags })` takes them, falling back to the guess per missing field. Titles are the first sentence, editable in place in the detail card. The book (`#book`, `renderBook`) lists every entry newest first and is two-way linked with the planet: hover a row to mark its tile, click to open it, click a tile to flash its row. `MI.world.highlightSlot/clearHighlight` marks a tile — vertex tint on the planet, gold rim plus a lift on the island — and `onHover` drives the pointer cursor.

Journal UI progress (2026-09-19): replaced the bottom entry bar with a prominent **Write a memory** button on the left, showing a gently rotating CSS miniature book. Opening the journal expands from the miniature's measured position and size into a centered two-page storybook. The book has a thicker cover, stacked page edges, spine shading and a teal ribbon bookmark; pages settle before writing, with reduced-motion support. Desktop shows past entries beside the lined textarea; phones use **Write a memory** / **Past entries** tabs and a sticky submit button. Enter adds a line, Ctrl/Cmd+Enter submits. Closing preserves the draft; failed submissions also retain it, and repeated submissions are guarded while busy. Changes are in `index.html` and `src/ui/ui.js`, with usage updated in `README.md`. Verified in a local browser at desktop and phone widths: opening/closing, typing, tags, submitting, draft preservation and keyboard focus. The current miniature is made with CSS, not a loaded 3D asset.

Your character and follow mode (built 2026-09-19, reworked 2026-09-20): the character is a **permanent inhabitant**, not a mode; it stands on the world in every view. **On the planet you only watch it** (no walking input, no follow button). **On the island, the arrow keys walk it** as you look down from orbit. The **footprints button** (icon only, a raised round puck at the right edge, shown only on a settled island) starts **follow mode**: the camera glides in (~0.85s tween, `tweenCamera`) to a fixed third-person view over the character's right shoulder, the mouse is captured (`requestPointerLock`) so moving it steers, **WASD walks camera-relative and the arrows do nothing**, the wheel is disabled and Esc (which the browser turns into a pointer-lock release, handled in `pointerlockchange`) glides back out. If pointer lock is refused, holding the middle mouse button still turns the camera. Follow is island-only; `setGroundView` returns false on the planet. Character and residents are about a third of a house's height (`PLAYER_SPHERE_SCALE`/`PLAYER_FLAT_SCALE`/`RESIDENT_FLAT_SCALE` in `world.js`). The camera is pulled in when it would sit inside a building (`eyeInsideBuilding`). The older description below still applies to the sphere maths (kept in `player.js`, tested, currently unreachable from input).

- **Terrain reads green** (2026-09-20): `other` is the keyword guess's catch-all and takes a large share of entries, so what it plants sets the tone of the whole island. It used to plant `dirt.glb`/`dirt-lumber.glb`, which turned most worlds brown; it now plants grass like the other everyday categories, and the brown and sand tiles are kept for memories that are actually about stone or travel. `grass-forest.glb` is a stand of eight trees spread over one tile, tall enough to swallow the follow camera, so it is one feature among several rather than the only one and `FEATURE_CHANCE` (0.3) keeps features rare. Tree tiles stop the CAMERA but not the character (`CANOPY_TILES`, `state.island.canopy`, `eyeBlocked`); making them solid would wall off whole tiles, which is a bigger call.
- **The island has levels** (2026-09-20): every tile's slab is 0.2 model units tall except the dirt family and water, which are half that, and a path laid on a tile adds 0.026 more. Walking at one fixed height therefore floated the character over dirt and buried its feet under paths. `state.island.groundY` holds each tile's walking height (`TILE_TOP`, `ROAD_THICKNESS`, built in `buildFlatView`); the character eases onto it (`easeGroundY`, `STEP_EASE`) so a change of level reads as a step rather than a snap, and residents interpolate between the tile they left and the one they are entering (`ctx.baseYOf`). Paths are also laid at their own tile's height now, instead of a fixed lift that left them hovering over the low ones.
- **Buildings are solid on the island** (2026-09-20): each building tile records a footprint BOX in `state.island.blockers` — `footprintBox` takes the model's parts that are neither the hex base plate nor clear above head height, and `islandBlocker` turns it by the tile's own rotation. A circle was wrong in both directions at once, holding you off the flat walls while letting you into the corners; tracing the whole model instead follows the roof, and the kit's houses are A-frames whose roof comes down to ankle height and reaches past the tile's flat edge, so a building may legitimately fill its tile (placement guarantees buildings are never adjacent, so walking around one is always possible). `moveFlat` in `player.js` refuses steps into a box (`blockedStep`) and slides along the wall's own axes, so a glancing approach glides and a head-on one stops dead against the wall, as in any game. `pushOut` steps a character back out of a box it is somehow inside — it spawns beside the house, under the reach of that roof. Roads, terrain and grass stay walkable; residents and pets are not blocked. Pebbles, logs, fences and other new props are deferred.
- **Movement** is continuous, not tile-to-tile (`src/world/player.js`, pure, `node scripts/test-player.js`). On the sphere a step ROTATES the position vector about `pos × move`: great-circle movement, so it never drifts off the unit sphere, has no pole singularity and needs no trig special cases. On the island it is a plain XZ translation. A blocked step is retried with each input axis alone, so walking into the sea slides you along the coast instead of sticking.
- **Facing is a vector, not an angle, and so is the ground camera's heading.** The first version stored both as angles measured from a "reference tangent" recomputed at each position. That tangent rotates as you move and flips outright near the poles, so the character and the camera both drifted on their own — the visible symptom was the camera swinging around you whenever you pressed A. Now a step reports the rotation it *is* (`lastAxis`/`lastAngle`) and everything with a direction is carried along by it (parallel transport). `test-player.js` holds that line: a carried direction must stay tangent and unit length, and nothing may jump as the character crosses a pole.
- **The sea stops you.** `MI.world.sphere.nearestSlot(dir)` is the tile under your feet — exact, not a guess, because the grid's tiles are the Voronoi cells of their own centres. The island uses the nearest coiled cell within its circumradius.
- **Everything is measured in TILES, not world units.** A tile's size changes with the planet's frequency and is different again on the island, so walking speed (`TILES_PER_SECOND`) and camera distance (`GROUND_DIST`) are both multiplied by the current view's tile size. Watch the unit: `state.spacing` is in the planet group's own units and that group is scaled by frequency/10, so anything the CAMERA uses goes through `groundTileSize()`, which converts to world space. Getting that wrong put the camera six times too far out the first time.
- **The camera** cannot reuse `updateCamera()`, which assumes +Y is up everywhere: on a sphere your up is wherever you are standing. `updateGroundCamera()` builds the eye from a basis on the character's own up.
- **W is always "away from the camera"**, in orbit as well as on the ground: `cameraAxes()` takes the real camera's direction each frame and projects it onto the ground at the character's feet.
- **The main house** (`MI.app.ensureHome`) claims one hexagon as land, with an oversized `building-house.glb` on it, when a world is first created — before, a fresh planet was all ocean and the character would have had nowhere to stand. Worlds that already have a `home` are left alone. The character spawns a third of a tile back from the house, *against* the camera's forward axis, so the house is in front of it rather than between it and the camera.
- **Characters** are one line each in `CHARACTERS` in `player.js`, chosen once rather than every time you look: `world.player.character` is persisted and loaded at boot. `MI.ui.openCharacterPicker()` is the way in — meant for the settings menu. The avatar choices use Kenney Mini Characters through the shared walker loader, with a procedural fallback if a model cannot load.
- Orbit's own controls are suspended, not changed, while on the ground: left-drag, hover-highlight and click-to-open all come back on the way out. Switching between planet and island leaves ground view first, since the two views hold the character in different places and the fold animates the camera.


Residents (merged from land-walker-pets, 2026-09-19): the people named in journal memories roam as autonomous walkers in planet and island views. Each person gets a stable Kenney Mini Characters model based on their person ID; the controllable avatar uses models from the same pack. Residents come from saved people, not shop purchases. src/world/walkers.js shares model loading and tile walking with pets. Residents prefer roads once they exist and can roam land before that.



Friends loiter (2026-09-20): a resident no longer hops between the tiles around their building. On each tile of one of their own memories they **meander freely within half a tile-width of its centre** — the hexagon's inscribed circle, so inside their own tile — picking a spot, walking to it, standing, picking another (`pickLoiterSpot`/`stepSpot`, pure, `node scripts/test-walkers.js`; spots are in tile-widths so one number holds at every planet size and in both views). A friend with several memories still walks the road between them, dwelling 3-5 pauses (`DWELL_ROUNDS`) at each stop before setting off; one memory means loitering on that one tile for good, and two memories with no road between them loiter where they are. On the road they walk the fixed "beside the building" spot (`RESIDENT_REST_*`) and ease to it when they leave a tile. Spots avoid a building: the island's footprint boxes (`state.island.blockers`), the middle of the tile on the planet (`RESIDENT_CORE`). **Kit houses on the island are 0.83-0.98 of a tile wide**, so there is almost no yard: if no spot fits within 0.5 they reach to 0.62 (`LOITER_WIDEN`), a strip around the house that runs a little over the tile's edge. A friend who arrives standing inside a footprint (the road's rest spot is) walks out by the nearest exit, about a second. `MI.world.__loiter()` reports each resident's spot for checking.

Placement (rebuilt 2026-09-20): the planet's land grows as a **compact blob around home**, like the island view, not a chain. `MI.placement.choose` (`src/world/placement.js`, pure, `node scripts/test-placement.js`) picks the free hexagon nearest home that joins existing land and touches **no other building (the house included)**; the memory's 1-3 dressing tiles then surround it. Growth remaps keep buildings apart (`nearestFree`'s `keepClearOf`) and now move `world.house.slot` with `home`; the island coil takes a `buildings` set so two houses are never side by side there either. Saved worlds keep their old tiles; only new memories use the rule. `world.heading` is unused now.

Skinned models and walk animation (2026-09-20): Mini Characters are skinned meshes, and `Object3D.clone()` leaves every copy driving the original's bones, which draws the copy at the world origin. `walkers.js` `cloneModel` rebuilds each copy's skeleton (r128 has no SkeletonUtils); use it for any skinned model.

- **The clips are already in the GLBs.** Every character model ships the same 31 clips — `idle`, `walk`, `sprint`, `jump`, `sit`, `emote-yes/no`, holding and attack poses, `wheelchair-*` — on one shared 7-bone skeleton (`root`, `leg-left`, `leg-right`, `torso`, `arm-left`, `arm-right`, `head`). The `walk` cycle is 0.67s, `idle` 1.33s. Nothing had to be authored; `makeAnimator` in `walkers.js` plays `walk` and `idle` together and cross-weights them (`BLEND_SECONDS`). The Cube Pets ship no clips, so they keep the `HOP_HEIGHT` bob, as does the procedural fallback figure.
- Clips are held in a **WeakMap (`clipsOf`), never on `userData`**: `Object3D.clone()` deep-copies userData through JSON, which would turn an `AnimationClip` into a plain object. `cloneModel` carries the entry to each clone, and clips bind by node name, so one loaded clip drives every copy through its own mixer.
- **The walk's pace follows how fast the figure actually moves**, in the model's own units (world distance ÷ the scale it is drawn at), so one setting holds at every planet size: `WALK_STRIDE` is the tuning knob, clamped by `MIN_RATE`/`MAX_RATE`. Residents get a random phase so a crowd doesn't march in lockstep.
- Only the visible instance's mixer advances (`isShown`); each view keeps its own copy. Mixers are disposed with the model (`disposeWalkerPair`, `clearAvatars`), or a replaced walker leaves a cached binding behind.
- Walking speed is `TILES_PER_SECOND` in `player.js` (0.7 — a character is about a third of a house tall now, so the old 1.7 read as a sprint).

## Where we are (2026-09-20)

Merged with `origin/main` at `5dab816`. That brought the team's UI work — the scrapbook shop
of ring-bound polaroids, cosmetics moved under settings, the journal as a 3D book that opens
and flips, a larger HUD — and then **several journals, each its own planet, switched between
in space** (`MI.store` library, `MI.app.rebuildScene/enterJournal/createJournal`,
`state.galaxy` in world.js, `node scripts/test-journals.js`). All of that stands as they
wrote it. The only thing kept from this side is the **footprints follow button**, which moved
to the right edge because the journal book now owns the bottom-right corner. Where the two
sides met: the follow camera keeps its fixed distance, so the wheel is the galaxy's alone;
clicks go to the galaxy first and only then to tiles; and `rebuildScene` claims the home tile
(`ensureHome`) so a started-over or brand-new journal has its house and somewhere to stand.

Built since: compact placement (the land grows as a blob, buildings never adjacent),
island-only follow mode with a third-person camera and mouse steering, characters played
through their own walk/idle clips at a third of a house's height, terrain back to green with
far fewer forests, and a first pass at solid buildings and tile levels.

**The 3D solidity and elevation work is NOT finished — it still does not look or play right.**
It is committed because the pieces underneath it (per-tile walking heights, footprint boxes,
push-out) are worth keeping and are covered by tests, not because the result is good enough.
What was verified was narrow: node tests, plus headless runs measuring feet against tile
heights and the gap to a wall. That is not the same as it feeling right to play, and it does
not. Treat the section below as a to-do, not a description of something working.

Known limits of that pass, and the likely places to look:
- **Only the island view has any of it.** The planet view has no levels, no solid buildings
  and no character control at all.
- **Only buildings are solid.** Trees, rocks, hills, fences and every other decoration are
  walked straight through. A `grass-forest` tile is eight trees across the whole tile; the
  follow camera avoids the canopy (`CANOPY_TILES`) but the character does not.
- **Height is per tile, not per point.** A tile is one flat level, so the raised middle of a
  hill, a dune or a mountain is walked through rather than climbed, and there are no real
  ramps or stairs anywhere — a step is a tile-to-tile change eased over a few frames.
- **A footprint box is one axis-aligned box per building**, from the parts below head height.
  It cannot describe an L-shape, a porch, a tower with a wide base, or anything you should be
  able to walk into rather than around.
- **Residents and pets ignore all of it** — they have no collision and stand at their tile's
  height only.
- The camera only pulls in for buildings and tree tiles; anything else can still come between
  it and the character.

Known gaps:
- Claude is no longer on the entry path: it only backs the detail card's "suggest a title" button, which hides itself when no key is configured. It has still never been seen answering — there is no local `.env` and the deployed key may not be set.
- Roads currently link ALL memories chronologically; residents roam the network, but paths are not yet limited to memories that share a person. With the compact layout the chronological roads are short hops, so this matters less visually.
- Follow mode is sightseeing: you cannot open a memory by walking up to it. The camera only avoids sitting inside a building; a building can still stand between it and the character.
- The planet has no character control at all (by design, for now).
